05 · Long description (~600 words)

copy 
Bundie is the prediction market for live trading agents on Solana.

The problem. AI trading agents are real economic actors now. Polystrat ran 4,200 trades on Polymarket in February 2026 with 37% positive PnL — multiples better than the human baseline. Dozens of trading agents are shipping monthly across Solana DeFi, with capital flowing into them. But the category has no fitness function. Every agent claims to be smart. None can prove it cheaply. Allocators fly blind. Operators starve. Bettors guess. Without a verifiable skill signal, the agent economy cannot compound.

The solution. Bundie hosts AI trading agents that execute real DeFi strategies on Solana, and runs binary prediction markets on their forward NAV. Bettors browse a leaderboard of agents ranked by realized 30-day return, drill into agent detail pages with live NAV charts and strategy summaries, and place YES/NO bets on whether an agent's NAV will cross a target threshold by a resolution date. When the chain reaches the resolution slot, Bundie reads the agent's NAV directly from on-chain state and settles the market. No human oracle, no committee, no off-chain data feed.

Why agents only. Prediction markets work when resolution is cheap and unambiguous. Polymarket pays humans to resolve general events. That model can't scale to thousands of agent strategies updating hourly. Trading agents are uniquely well-suited to self-resolving markets because their strategy is on-chain, their NAV is deterministic, and their performance can be read from chain state at any slot. Random wallets don't qualify. Humans don't qualify. Agents do.

Why Solana. Bundie uses Solana primitives that don't exist on other chains. SNS resolves .sol names as on-chain agent identity (kamino-stacker.bundie.sol carries a verifiable performance track record). Sub-second slot times let Bundie compute NAV at intervals fine enough for active prediction markets, not just daily snapshots. Custom Anchor programs implement Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule market makers over continuous on-chain NAV. The chaos-sim daemon executes agent strategies via direct SDK integrations into Marinade, Kamino, Solend, Jito's SPL stake pool, Jupiter, and Jupiter Perps — no third-party CPI middleware. Seeker TWA wraps the existing Next.js app for mobile distribution.

What's shipped. Live on devnet at solana.bundie.fi. Anchor programs deployed (LS-LMSR market maker, NAV oracle, settlement, bUSD escrow). Six trading agents running in production: kamino-stacker (Kamino USDC lending), apy-rotator (yield rotation across lending venues), funding-shorter (Jupiter Perps funding-rate carry), stable-arber (cross-protocol stable yield aggregation), barbell (50/50 Kamino + perp long), lst-shopper (Marinade/Jito LST yield comparison). Real bUSD flowing through devnet. Discover, Markets, Agent detail, Market detail, Portfolio, and Wallet routes all functional with the new dark editorial design.

Business model. 1% on settlement volume, listing fees from agent operators, and a NAV data API as a third revenue line. TAM is $2.4B annual fee pool at 1% of Bernstein's $240B projected 2026 prediction market volume. SOM by year three is $4.5–7.5M ARR.

The category claim. Bundie sits between the agent layer (Lulo, Breeze, Drift, Kamino, Almanak run agents) and the prediction market layer (Polymarket settles via humans). Nobody else operates at the intersection of betting on agent NAV. Defensibility comes from owning the NAV-stream schema — the canonical format the agent economy publishes performance in. Bundie is racing to become that layer before Polymarket decides this niche is worth their direct attention.

Mainnet target: May 2026.

09 · Solana-native execution

copy 
Bundie uses Solana primitives that don't exist on other chains. This is core to the product, not a bolt-on.

- Custom Anchor programs. LS-LMSR market makers over continuous on-chain NAV. Implements Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule for binary outcome markets with NAV-based resolution.
- SNS Identity. .sol names serve as on-chain agent identity. Each agent gets a verifiable performance track record tied to its SNS handle.
- Sub-second NAV streams. Solana's slot times let Bundie compute agent NAV at intervals fine enough for active intraday markets. Not possible on chains with 12+ second block times.
- Direct DeFi integrations. The chaos-sim daemon executes agent strategies via direct SDK calls into Marinade, Kamino, Solend, Jito SPL stake pool, Jupiter, and Jupiter Perps — no third-party middleware. Each agent's positions are read from chain state for NAV computation.
- Seeker TWA. Mobile distribution via the existing Next.js app opened in the Seeker browser. Same code, no separate build.
- Solana Wallet Adapter for wallet connection, SPL token program for bUSD settlement.

A wallet-connect button is not a Solana product. Bundie's settlement mechanism, market-making, identity layer, and execution layer are all Solana-native.

18 · Q&A prep — what judges will probe

Calibrated to the current product (no Beethoven, no mirror-agent framing).

"How do you compete with Polymarket's MOOV2?"

Polymarket's MOOV2 is a managed-human-resolution architecture. It works for general events at the per-market scale Polymarket already operates. It does not scale to thousands of agent strategies updating hourly because every market needs a human attestation step. Our resolution is deterministic chain-state read — zero marginal cost per market. That's a structural advantage at agent-economy scale.

"Liquidity capture / market making — who provides the float?"

v1: treasury-subsidized LMSR. The protocol seeds initial liquidity into each market on creation; the LS-LMSR's mathematical structure means the worst-case loss to the seeder is bounded by the initial liquidity parameter. Post-launch, fee revenue replenishes the treasury. v2: pm-AMM lets external LPs provide liquidity and capture trading fees.

"Why agents and not any wallet's NAV?"

Because we need deterministic NAV. A trading agent's strategy is on-chain (the protocol calls are public, the positions are decodable). A random wallet's NAV is whatever the wallet chooses to report — there's no way to know if the wallet is a person, a fund, a hot wallet for a CEX. Agents are the subset where on-chain state alone reconstructs the NAV. That's the qualifier.

"Where does the market-maker expertise come from?"

If Sean is on the team: Sean designed the LS-LMSR implementation; previously [insert background]. If Sean is not on the team: we're advised by [pipeline] and the LS-LMSR is a well-studied market structure with public reference implementations we adapted for Solana's compute-unit constraints.

"Have you actually tested settlement end-to-end?"

Yes — devnet markets resolve via the on-chain settlement instruction. Show a resolved market's tx if a judge presses. (If no resolved market exists at submission time, force-resolve one before recording the demo.)

"What's the difference between your agents and a yield aggregator?"

An aggregator allocates user deposits across pools by rules. Our agents are autonomous brains: each tick, they observe live mainnet rate surfaces (Kamino utilization, Marinade mSOL above-par, Jupiter Perps funding), reason about a strategy expressed in their brain.md, and emit on-chain actions through direct SDK calls. The brain is an LLM (Anthropic via Redpill) prompted with each agent's strategy. The output is a BrainDecision the executor signs and broadcasts. That's what makes them trading agents — they make context-dependent decisions, not template allocations.

"Why didn't you ship docs?"

Honest answer: with a 4-day mainnet runway, maintaining a docs surface that drifts from the live product is a worse signal than no docs at all. README is canonical for builders; the live app is canonical for bettors; the deck is canonical for investors. Each has a single owner and gets updated when the product moves. Post-mainnet we'll ship a real docs surface tied to the public schema.
